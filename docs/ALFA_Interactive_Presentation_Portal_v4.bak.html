Total per student = $250 + master rate × $2,500 · Annual cost = total × 400 · Saving at 65% direct = total × 260 · Net saving = saving − $42,000

The plan saves more money when the commission is higher, so our conclusion is correct for any of these rates. We use 5% because it keeps the total at 15%, which is at the top of the typical 10 to 15% industry range for higher education (QualyHQ, 2026; GradPilot, 2026). The 5% and the split are our assumption, not an official ALFA figure.

Sources and assumptions
  • QualyHQ. (2026). How education agent commissions work: Rates, gross vs net, and getting paid on time. qualyhq.com

  • Industry guides say sub-agents often keep 50% to 70% of the commission. Our split is about 67% (sub-agent) and 33% (master agent, our assumption).
  • Our assumptions: 12 master agents × 8 sub-agents, 400 new students, 120 staff, $2,500 first-year fee, 5% master-agent rate, $10,000 current marketing spend, 5–7 day admin wait (our estimate). ALFA does not publish these numbers.
  • OneHub is our proposal, not an existing ALFA system. The website offers a MyClass login, but applications, payment proof and many staff processes still run on forms and email (our observation).
